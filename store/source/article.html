Keep your notes where they last

Lena Ortiz · October 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Every note-taking app makes the same quiet promise: write here and you will find it again. Apps come and go, though, and the notes inside them tend to leave when they do. The notes that survive are the ones kept in a format that needs no particular app at all.

Plain text is that format. A Markdown file opens in any editor on any machine, today and in twenty years. It syncs with whatever you already use, it diffs cleanly, and it never asks you to export your own thinking.

Write first, organize later

The best capture habit is the one with the least friction. Jot the idea, the quote, the link you meant to read, and give it a name. Folders and links can come later, once you know what the note is for.

A note you can still open in ten years is worth more than a perfect one you cannot.

A few small habits make a folder of text files feel like a working library:

  • Put the date and the source at the top of every note.
  • Link related notes with [[double brackets]].
  • Keep clippings in their own folder until you have read them.

Let the tools come and go

When your notes are just files, switching tools stops being a migration. You open the same folder in something new, and everything you wrote is still there, exactly as you left it.
